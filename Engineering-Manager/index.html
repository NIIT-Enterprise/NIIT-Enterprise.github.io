Program Overview

An Engineering Manager program, not an AI enablement program

Program Design · Foundation Layer → Engineering Manager Pathway → Capstone, with a separate AI Value Realisation Track.

01

Program Positioning

An Engineering Manager program, not an AI enablement program. It prepares experienced PMs/PGMs and relevant technical professionals to transition into Engineering Manager roles, able to manage technology, engineering, AI-enabled delivery and business outcomes. AI is an important, embedded component throughout: participants learn how AI is built into engineering and delivery environments, not just how to use AI productivity tools.

Context

Engineering leaders are expected to drive not only delivery and team performance, but informed decisions on architecture, data, AI, engineering productivity, quality, governance and value realisation. Current PM/PGM roles remain focused on timelines, cost, resources, customer management and delivery governance. The program builds a new generation of Engineering Managers who bridge business, customer, technology and engineering teams (see Context tab).

02

Learning Objectives
  • Redefine the Engineering Manager role for the AI era: from tracking people, cost and timelines to leading technology, engineering, AI-enabled delivery and business outcomes.
  • Build the technical fluency to make sound decisions on architecture (enterprise and agentic), platforms, data and AI models, and to work as a credible partner to architects and engineers.
  • Redesign how teams build software with an AI-native SDLC, quality engineering for probabilistic systems, platform engineering and measurable engineering productivity and developer experience.
  • Lead and govern AI-enabled delivery: agents and agent management, human-in-the-loop, orchestration, AI governance, privacy, security, risk controls and escalation.
  • Think at enterprise level by connecting technology decisions to reliability, compliance, security, resilience, integration, cost and client outcomes.
  • Lead human + AI teams across generations and geographies with developer empathy, clear decision rights, delegation and sound judgement.

05

Assumptions
  • This is an Engineering Manager transition program, not an AI enablement program. AI is embedded throughout as a core component, but the objective is to build the full capability set of the Engineering Manager role.
  • Target participants are experienced PMs/PGMs and relevant technical professionals with real project and delivery exposure, selected through a pre-program assessment against defined entry criteria.
  • The program works as a funnel: an assessment gate routes candidates to the EM Pathway, to a Foundation Layer (for those with mainly traditional PM responsibilities such as reporting, timesheets and people tracking) or to a separate AI Value Realisation Track (for PMs/PGMs already running AI-rich projects who are not EM candidates).
  • The EM Pathway is ~80 hours over 9 weeks: an optional 2-day boot camp (14 hrs) followed by four sprints at ~8–10 hours per week, moving from frame and fluency to architecture, engineering, governance and leadership.
  • The program does not reteach basic project management or teach AI tools and prompting for their own sake. Tools are used inside realistic engineering and delivery scenarios.
  • Each sprint follows Impart → Immerse → Introspect: concept sessions, then an enterprise case analysis, then participants apply the sprint to their own engagement and present it.
  • Human accountability remains explicit. AI outputs are treated as evidence and options to be validated, not as authority.
  • If the boot camp is not run, its 14 hours are delivered as live virtual sessions in Weeks 1–2 and the schedule extends by one week.

Engineering Manager Pathway

Detailed Design — ~80 hours · 9 weeks

Optional 2-day boot camp + four sprints at ~8–10 hrs/week · Impart → Immerse → Introspect in every stage. Select any session to open its purpose, coverage, hands-on activity, learning objectives and rationale.

Legend — Impart Concept and practice sessions that build a capability area.   Immerse Enterprise case analysis or simulation that integrates the stage's capability areas.   Introspect Participants apply the stage to their own engagement and present it for panel feedback.

1The Engineering Manager in the AI Era: From Delivery Manager to Engineering LeaderImpartEngineering Leadership – Decisions, Delegation & Judgement · In-person boot camp · Week 13 hrs▾
Purpose
Establish what changes when a delivery or program manager becomes an Engineering Manager, and what the AI era adds to the role.
Suggested Coverage
PM vs EM role; people, delivery, cost and timeline management vs technology, engineering and outcome leadership; managing AI-enabled teams and agents; outcome-based delivery; orchestration; technical decision rights; engineering credibility; EM role and client expectations; personal capability gaps.
Hands-on Activity
Role Reclassification: sort current weekly responsibilities into Retain, Automate, Delegate (human/agent) and New EM Responsibility, then compare with peers.
3 Key Learning Objectives
  1. Articulate how the EM role differs from the current PM/PGM role.
  2. Identify the decisions an EM owns that a PM typically escalates.
  3. Map personal strengths and gaps against the program's 11 capability areas.
Rationale
Positions the program as an EM transition from the first hour and prevents it from being read as a PM course or an AI tools course.
2AI Fluency for Engineering Managers: How LLMs, RAG and Agents Really WorkImpartAI Fluency for Engineering Managers · In-person boot camp · Week 14 hrs▾
Purpose
Give participants a working mental model of modern AI systems so they can make and challenge engineering decisions involving AI.
Suggested Coverage
ML vs GenAI; LLM mechanics (tokens, context windows, temperature); hallucination and non-determinism; prompt and context engineering; RAG; tool use; agents and multi-agent patterns; AI coding assistants; model families; what AI cannot do reliably; productivity tools vs AI-embedded systems.
Hands-on Activity
AI Build Lab: in pairs, build a small RAG assistant and a simple tool-using agent with low-code tooling, then break it deliberately and log the failure modes.
3 Key Learning Objectives
  1. Explain in plain language how an LLM, a RAG pipeline and an agent produce outputs.
  2. Identify failure modes typical of AI components.
  3. Distinguish using AI productivity tools from leading delivery of AI-embedded systems.
Rationale
As per our discussion, it was explicit that the program must go beyond AI tools and prompting. Hands-on build-and-break gives non-specialists real intuition fast.
3Platform Thinking and the Digital Business Technology Platform (DBTP)ImpartPlatform Thinking (DBTP) · In-person boot camp · Week 13 hrs▾
Purpose
Help participants see their engagements as part of a platform landscape and use platforms to drive speed, reuse and quality.
Suggested Coverage
DBTP layers (experience, AI/data, integration, core systems, infrastructure); platform as product; shared services and APIs; reuse vs bespoke build; cloud-native basics; platform economics; platform teams vs stream-aligned teams (Team Topologies); impact on client value.
Hands-on Activity
Platform Canvas: map a current client engagement onto the DBTP layers and highlight duplication, gaps and reuse opportunities.
3 Key Learning Objectives
  1. Describe the DBTP layers and where their current engagement sits.
  2. Identify reuse and platform opportunities that would change delivery speed or cost.
  3. Explain how team structure should follow platform architecture.
Rationale
Platform thinking is the first capability in the Design tab and the anchor for later architecture and platform engineering sessions.
4Experience Thinking: Customer Experience, Developer Experience and Developer EmpathyImpartExperience Thinking (Customer & Developer Experience) · In-person boot camp · Week 12 hrs▾

Process and Timeline

2–3 weeks

  • Week 2: Eligible candidates complete the Self-Assessment, Knowledge Test and Situational Judgement Test online.
  • Week 2–3: Shortlisted candidates (or all eligible, for cohorts under 40) present the case to a panel of 2–3 (NIIT faculty + client engineering leader).
  • Week 3: Calibration meeting reviews the Scorecard, confirms routes and shares individual feedback reports.
  • Re-use: the same rubric is applied at program end to measure capability growth (Kirkpatrick Level 2).
